e-desk — field services management

Ongoing · Running in production

A platform that runs small trades businesses — jobs, engineers, costs, invoicing. I redesigned the jobsheet, the screen where office staff and field engineers meet.

The problem
The page held every piece of data it needed and still didn't answer the question people opened it for: who did what, on which day, and what it cost. It was organised around the database rather than around the day.
The constraint
I couldn't change the colour scheme, the typeface or the component style, and it was a live React build. Structure and hierarchy were the only levers I had. That turned out to be enough.
What I owned
Page structure, hierarchy, every state, and the handoff spec the developers built from.
What changed
The day became the unit the page is organised around, with cost visible beside the work rather than summarised elsewhere. It shipped and it's in production.

About

I've spent most of my career inside products where the screen is the job — where someone opens it at nine in the morning and is still in it at six. That work rewards a particular kind of attention: deciding what is genuinely less important, drawing the states nobody enjoys drawing, and resisting the urge to make a thing prettier when it needs to be clearer.

I studied UX/UI design at Yandex Practicum, and I've worked with cross-cultural teams across several time zones. I work independently and asynchronously — short recordings, comments left in the file, and work waiting when you start your day.

Most of my engagements come through Upwork, where my work history and client reviews are public.
